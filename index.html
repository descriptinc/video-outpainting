Video Outpainting

Reframing video onto any canvas by generating the world beyond the frame

Matthew Bendel Vicki Anand Stephen W. Bailey Mithilesh Vaidya Sumukh Badam Xingzhe He

Descript, Inc.

ATS project page ↗ ATS paper ↗

Footage shot for one screen rarely fits another: cropping throws picture away, and letterboxing leaves black bars. Outpainting keeps the whole source and generates the canvas around it, holding the new borders steady for the full length of the video. Long videos are possible because of Anchored Tree Sampling.

Examples

Pick a clip and flip between Original (the source letterboxed on the new canvas) and Outpainted. Everything outside the original frame is generated.

Method

Outpainting is inpainting with the mask on the outside. The source is placed on the new canvas and kept untouched, and a pretrained video-to-video generator fills in everything around it. The hard part is doing that consistently for minutes at a time.

Anchored Tree Sampling

The model generates a few seconds at a time, so long videos are usually built by autoregressive rollout: each chunk is conditioned on the one before it. Errors compound, and the borders slowly drift. Next to source footage that never changes, that drift is easy to see.

ATS fills the timeline sparse to dense instead. A root call places anchor frames across the whole video, guidance calls add anchors between them, and leaf calls fill each span between neighboring anchors. Every call after the root is bracketed by anchors on both sides, so drift stays bounded no matter how long the video is. Calls on the same level are independent and run in parallel.

The tree that unlocks long generation

Goodbye Drift: Anchored Tree Sampling for Long-Horizon Video-to-Video Generation

A training-free scheduler for any two-sided video-to-video generator. The paper shows stable 40+ minute generations across five conditioning modalities, outpainting included, and a 5.3× speedup over the strongest autoregressive baseline at a 2000 s horizon.
